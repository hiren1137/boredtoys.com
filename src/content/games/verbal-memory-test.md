## How To Play the Verbal Memory Test

Press Start and a single word appears on the card, like "kite" or "pancake". Your only job is to decide if you have already seen that word in this game.

- Press **Seen** if the word came up earlier in this run.
- Press **New** if this is the first time it has shown up.

The very first word is always new, so that one is a free point. After that it gets trickier. Some words will be repeats and some will be brand new, all mixed together.

Every right answer adds one point to your score. A wrong answer costs one of your three hearts. Lose all three and the game ends.

You can tap the buttons, click them, or use the keyboard. Press S for Seen and N for New, or use the left and right arrow keys.

## What Your Score Means

Your score is the number of words you judged correctly before running out of lives. Higher is better.

- **Under 10 words:** a warm-up. The first few repeats tend to catch everyone off guard.
- **10 to 24 words:** a solid start. You are keeping a decent mental list.
- **25 to 49 words:** strong. You are tracking a lot of words at once.
- **50 to 79 words:** impressive. Your memory for words is really holding up.
- **80 words or more:** outstanding. Very few runs make it this far.

Your score can change a lot from one game to the next. Being tired, distracted or in a hurry makes a big difference, so try a few rounds before you decide how good you are.

## Tips To Improve

- **Picture each word.** When "lantern" shows up, imagine a glowing lantern for a second. A quick picture sticks better than the plain letters.
- **Trust the first feeling.** That little spark of "I know you" is usually right. Overthinking tends to turn a correct Seen into a wrong New.
- **Watch for look-alikes.** Words like "pear" and "peach" or "boat" and "goat" are easy to mix up. Read the whole word before you answer.
- **Take a breath after a mistake.** One wrong answer often leads to another if you rush. Slow down for the next word.
- **Play when you are fresh.** Your memory works best when you are rested and not distracted.

## Why Words Feel Familiar

Psychologists often split memory into two jobs. Recall is pulling something out of your head with no help, like naming every word from a list. Recognition is spotting something when you see it again. Recognition is usually much easier, which is why you can feel sure you saw "giraffe" a minute ago even though you could never have listed every word in order.

This game tests recognition. The hard part is that every word starts to feel a bit familiar after a while, so you have to tell "I really saw this" apart from "this just feels known".

If you want to test the recall side, try the [number memory test](/number-memory-test/), where you have to type a number back from memory.
