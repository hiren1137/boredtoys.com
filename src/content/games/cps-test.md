## How To Play the CPS Test

First, pick how long you want the test to last. The chips at the top let you choose 1, 5, 10, 30 or 60 seconds. Five seconds is picked for you.

Then click the big red button. Your first click starts the timer, so there is no countdown to wait for. The button turns green, and every click after that adds one to your count. Keep going as fast as you can until the timer hits zero.

When time is up, the button turns yellow and stops counting for a moment. That way one last wild click does not skip past your result. Then you see your clicks per second, your total clicks and your best score for that test length.

On a phone, tap with your finger. On a computer, use your mouse, or press Space or Enter on the keyboard.

## What Your Score Means

Your score is your total clicks divided by the number of seconds. Higher is better, and each test length keeps its own best score, so a 1 second sprint never overwrites your 60 second record.

- **Under 5 CPS:** a relaxed pace. Most people land here on their first try.
- **5 to 7 CPS:** a quick, steady clicker.
- **7 to 10 CPS:** properly fast. Your mouse button is working hard.
- **Over 10 CPS:** very fast, usually from a special clicking style or a lot of practice.

Short tests give higher scores than long ones. Your hand gets tired, so a 60 second score is almost always lower than a 5 second score. Compare your scores only with the same test length.

## Tips To Improve

- **Relax your hand.** A tense grip slows your finger down. Keep your wrist loose and let the finger do the work.
- **Use small movements.** Lift your finger just enough for the button to click again. Big movements waste time.
- **Try two fingers on a touch screen.** Tapping with your index and middle finger in turn can beat a single finger.
- **Find a rhythm.** A steady beat is faster over 10 seconds or more than bursts with pauses.
- **Warm up first.** A practice round or two loosens your hand before you go for a record.

## Clicking Styles and a Word on Safety

Fast clickers often talk about two styles. Jitter clicking means tensing your arm so your finger shakes on the button very quickly. Butterfly clicking means tapping one button with two fingers, one after the other. Both can push your clicks per second higher than normal clicking.

Be kind to your body and your gear. Jitter clicking puts a lot of strain on your wrist and arm, so stop if anything hurts. Very hard clicking can also wear out a mouse button. A few fast rounds are fun, but you do not need to click for hours to see real progress.
