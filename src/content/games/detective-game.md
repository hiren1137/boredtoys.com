## How To Play the Detective Game

Something has gone wrong. Maybe the last slice of cake vanished, or the class hamster escaped, or somebody drew mustaches on the class photo. Your job is to figure out who did it.

Every case shows a short scene, four suspects and one clue. Look at each suspect carefully. Notice their hat, their hair, their glasses, their shirt color and pattern, and whether they have a mustache. Read what they say, too, because their own words often give them away.

Each clue knocks out at least one suspect. Use the Rule Out button to grey out anyone who cannot be the culprit. If you are not sure yet, press Reveal Clue to get another hint. When you know who did it, press Accuse.

After each accusation you see who the real culprit was and what actually happened. Then it is on to the next case, until all 12 are solved or spectacularly unsolved.

## What Your Score Means

Your score is the total number of stars you earn across all 12 cases. The most you can get is 36.

- **30 to 36 stars:** you accuse early and you are almost always right. Great detectives do not need every clue.
- **20 to 29 stars:** careful and clever. You know when to take a risk and when to wait.
- **12 to 19 stars:** you solve plenty of cases, but you often wait for the last clue or make a few wrong guesses.
- **Under 12 stars:** the culprits got lucky this time. Slow down and read every quote.

Your best total is saved on your device, so you can come back and try to break your own record.

## Tips To Improve

- **Count the survivors.** After each clue, ask how many suspects still fit. If only one is left, accuse right away for the most stars.
- **Read the quotes.** Suspects often tell you where they were or what they were doing. That matters when a clue says the culprit was inside, awake or holding a certain paint color.
- **Use Rule Out.** Greying out cleared suspects makes the last one easy to spot, especially on a small screen.
- **Check every detail.** A bow counts as a hat. Stripes and polka dots are different patterns. Glasses and mustaches are easy to miss.
- **Guess smart.** If two suspects are left and you feel brave, accusing now can earn an extra star. If you want to be safe, reveal one more clue.

## Why Whodunit Puzzles Feel So Good

Mystery stories have been popular for well over a hundred years, and the fun is always the same: you collect small facts and suddenly they click together. Puzzles like these are a gentle kind of logic training. Each clue removes possibilities until only one answer is left, which is the same idea people use to fix a broken bike, find a missing sock or debug a computer program.

These cases are short on purpose. You can solve one in under a minute, and none of the culprits did anything worse than eat too many cookies.
