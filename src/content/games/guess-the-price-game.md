## How To Play the Guess the Price Game

Each round shows one famous thing that really sold at auction. You get the name, a one-line description and the year it was made or found, not the price.

Move the slider until the dollar amount feels right, then press **Place bid**. The slider runs from one thousand dollars to about one billion, and each step multiplies the price instead of adding a flat amount. That is so a dinosaur skeleton and a painting can share one control.

After you bid, the game shows the reported sale price, the auction house and the year. Your points land underneath. Then the next lot appears. A full game is 10 lots, picked from a longer list, so a second play is not the same set.

## What Your Score Means

Every lot is worth up to 1,000 points, so a perfect game is 10,000.

- **An exact guess:** all 1,000 points for that lot.
- **Off by about ten times:** you still earn some points. Guessing 5 million on a 50 million sale is in the game.
- **Off by about a hundred times:** that lot is worth zero. A million-dollar guess on a hundred-million-dollar painting misses the scale.

A score above 7,000 means your guesses kept the right number of zeros. A score under 4,000 means a few lots jumped a whole category of money. Your best total is saved on your device. The share button uses that total, not a made-up rank.

## Tips To Improve

- **Think in jumps, not dollars.** Ask if it sold for thousands, millions or hundreds of millions before you fine-tune.
- **Bones and jewels are not the same as paintings.** The most expensive paintings in this list sit far above a famous fossil.
- **Read the year.** A sale from the 1990s can look cheap next to one from the 2020s, even when the object is legendary.
- **Play twice.** The second round drops some lots you just learned and brings in others.

## Where the Prices Come From

Each figure is a reported auction result, not a private handshake and not a guess. The reveal names the house: Christie's, Sotheby's or Julien's Auctions. When a report rounded the number, the note on the result says "about." Nothing here is a prediction of what the same object would sell for tomorrow. Prices move, and a second sale of a similar thing can land far from the number in this list.
