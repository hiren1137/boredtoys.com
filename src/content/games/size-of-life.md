## How To Play Size of Life

You start face to face with a flu virus, blown up so big it fills the screen. Above it you see its name, how big it really is and one fun fact.

Press **Next** and the camera zooms out to show the next living thing. The one you just met shrinks next to it, so you can see how they compare at true scale. A little tag points at the smaller one so you never lose it.

Keep going and the jumps get wild. A red blood cell becomes a speck next to a paramecium. A cat towers over a hedgehog. A blue whale makes the biggest fish in the sea look small. The last stop is bigger than a whole town.

You can move in three ways:

- Tap the **Next** and **Back** buttons.
- Swipe left or right on a phone or tablet.
- Press the right and left arrow keys on a keyboard.

The ruler in the bottom corner changes as you zoom. It switches from nanometers to micrometers, millimeters, centimeters, meters and finally kilometers.

## What Your Score Means

There is no time limit and no way to lose. Your score is how many living things you have zoomed through, and the trip has 32 stops in all. Reaching the end means you have traveled from about 100 nanometers to about 3.8 kilometers. That is roughly 38 billion times bigger.

Sizes are measured the way scientists usually describe each one. Tall things like people, giraffes and trees use height. Long things like ants, whales and crocodiles use body length, and tiny round things like cells use width. Every number is a typical or record value, rounded to keep it simple. Real individuals can be a fair bit bigger or smaller.

## Tips To Improve

- **Read the fact before you move on.** Each stop has a surprise, like the giraffe with the same number of neck bones as you.
- **Watch the ruler.** It shows how fast the numbers grow better than anything else on the screen.
- **Go back and forth.** Pressing Back and Next a few times makes the size jumps really sink in.
- **Look for the tag.** When the smaller creature turns into a speck, the tag still shows where it is standing.
- **Guess first.** Before each zoom, try to guess how much bigger the next one will be.

## Fun Facts About the Size of Life

Living things span a huge range of sizes. Most of them are far too small to see. You need a microscope to see a single bacterium, yet a pinch of garden soil can hold millions of them.

At the other end, the biggest living things are not always animals. A coast redwood can grow taller than a 30-story building. In Utah, a grove of aspen trees called Pando shares one root system and counts as a single plant. In Oregon, a honey fungus spreads its threads underground through about 9.6 square kilometers of forest. Most of it stays hidden, and only small clusters of mushrooms ever pop up above the ground.
