## How To Play Spend Jeff Bezos' Money

Imagine logging in to your bank and seeing $369 billion. No bills to pay, no rules, just one mission: spend it. The green counter at the top of the game shows exactly how much is left, and it follows you down the page while you shop.

Each card in the shop has a price, a **Buy** button, a **Sell** button and a little number box. Tap Buy to add one, tap Sell to give one back, or type a number to order a whole pile at once. Want 300 delivery vans? Type 300. You can never spend more than you have. Ask for too many and the game hands you as many as you can afford, with a small wiggle so you notice.

Your receipt appears under the shop as soon as you buy something. It lists every item, how many you bought and the total, and it has buttons to share your spree.

## What Your Score Means

Your score is how much money you managed to spend. Here is a rough feel for each level:

- **Under $10,000:** smart speakers, an e-reader, a puppy and a hot tub. A great weekend, and the counter did not even blink.
- **Millions:** a private island, a superyacht and a rocket launch. Fancy, but still pocket change at this scale.
- **Billions:** a movie studio, a moon lander program and a whole grocery store chain. Now the number is finally moving.
- **Over $300 billion:** you are buying pizza for the entire planet more than once. Zero is in sight.

Your biggest spree is saved on your device as your personal best.

## Tips To Improve

- **Start at the bottom.** The most expensive cards sit at the end of the shop. Scroll down first and work your way up.
- **Use the number box.** Typing 50 into a big card is much faster than tapping Buy fifty times.
- **Finish with snacks.** When only a few hundred dollars are left, donuts and coffees let you land exactly on $0.

## Where the Number and Prices Come From

The starting amount is Jeff Bezos' estimated net worth from the Forbes Real-Time Billionaires list on October 27, 2026. Most of his wealth is in shares of the online store he founded in 1994, so the figure rises and falls with the stock market. We pull a fresh number once a week.

Several shop items are based on real purchases and pledges that were widely reported. In 2013 Bezos bought a major US daily newspaper for $250 million. In 2020 he paid about $165 million for a Beverly Hills estate, a record for the area at the time, and he pledged $10 billion to a climate fund. He has put about $42 million into a giant clock built inside a mountain in Texas, designed to keep ticking for 10,000 years.

The company he founded paid about $13.7 billion for a grocery chain in 2017 and about $8.5 billion for an old Hollywood movie studio in 2022. His rocket company won a NASA moon lander contract worth about $3.4 billion in 2023. Other prices, like the warehouse and the space station module, are round guesses at what those things cost to build.
