## How To Play the Typing Speed Test

Pick a test length first: 15, 30 or 60 seconds. The 60 second test is the classic choice, and the short ones are great for a quick warm up.

Click or tap the word box, then start typing the gray words. The clock only starts when you press your first key, so take a breath before you go. Press space after each word to jump to the next one.

Letters turn dark when you get them right and red when you get them wrong. A red line under a word means it had a mistake. You can fix the word you are on with backspace, but once you press space, that word is locked in.

When time runs out, you see your words per minute, your accuracy and a few more stats. Press Restart or Esc any time to start over with fresh words.

## What Your Score Means

WPM stands for words per minute. Typing tests count every 5 characters as one word, which is a widely used standard. It keeps scores fair, so typing "cat" doesn't count the same as typing "mountain". Spaces count as characters too.

Only words you type correctly add to your WPM. That's why your result shows two speeds. WPM is your clean speed, and raw speed counts every key, mistakes and all.

- **Under 20 WPM:** you are still finding the keys. Everyone starts here.
- **20 to 39 WPM:** a steady pace. You can write messages without much fuss.
- **40 to 59 WPM:** comfy, confident typing for school or work.
- **60 to 79 WPM:** quick. Your fingers know where they are going.
- **80 to 99 WPM:** very fast. This takes a lot of practice.
- **100 WPM and up:** serious speed. Time to brag a little.

Your keyboard matters. A big desk keyboard, a laptop and a phone screen all feel different, so compare scores from the same device.

## Tips To Improve Your WPM

- **Keep your eyes on the words.** Looking down at your hands slows you down more than anything else.
- **Use all ten fingers.** Rest your fingers on the middle row of letters and reach out from there.
- **Go for clean, then fast.** Mistakes cost you twice: once for the wrong key and again for fixing it.
- **Read one word ahead.** While your fingers type this word, your eyes should already be on the next one.
- **Stay relaxed.** Tight shoulders and a death grip on the desk make your fingers stiff.
- **Practice a little every day.** A few short tests daily beat one long session once a week.

## Fun Facts About Typing

Early typewriters were slow and clunky. Their keys could jam if two neighbors were hit too fast, and some people think that helped shape the odd order of letters we still use today. Fast typing contests have been around for more than a hundred years, long before computers.

Today you type all day without thinking about it: messages, search boxes, homework and games. A few minutes of practice here can save you hours over a year.
