## How To Play Spend Mark Zuckerberg's Money

You have just been handed $216 billion. Your only job is to get rid of it. The green counter at the top of the game shows how much is left, and it stays pinned to the screen while you scroll through the shop.

Every card has a price, a **Buy** button, a **Sell** button and a small number box. Tap Buy to add one. Tap Sell to return one. Or type a number to grab a whole stack, like 40 VR headsets for the office. If you ask for more than you can afford, the game gives you the most you can pay for and wiggles the card so you notice.

As soon as you buy something, a receipt shows up under the shop. It lists each item, how many you got and your total, plus buttons to share your shopping spree.

## What Your Score Means

Your score is the amount you spent. Here is how each level feels:

- **Under $10,000:** a hoodie, smart glasses, a puppy and a year of MMA lessons. Nice, but the counter barely flinched.
- **Millions:** a cattle herd, an underground shelter and a private jet. Fancy, and still tiny at this scale.
- **Billions:** a photo app, a VR company and a pro football team. Now the number really starts to drop.
- **Over $200 billion:** you are ordering pizza for the whole planet. The finish line is close.

Your biggest spree is saved on this device as your personal best.

## Tips To Improve

- **Shop from the bottom up.** The priciest cards are at the end of the list. Start there and work back toward the snacks.
- **Type big numbers.** Putting 10 into a data center card is faster than tapping Buy ten times.
- **Save small change for last.** Coffees, donuts and socks are perfect for wiping out the final few dollars.

## Where the Number and Prices Come From

The starting amount is Mark Zuckerberg's estimated net worth from the Forbes Real-Time Billionaires list on November 12, 2026. Most of it is tied up in shares of the social media company he started in his college dorm in 2004, so the figure moves with the stock market. We update it once a week.

Some shop items are based on real deals. His company bought a photo-sharing app for about $1 billion in 2012, then a VR headset maker for about $2 billion and a messaging app for about $19 billion in 2014. In 2016 he and his wife pledged $3 billion toward science aimed at curing diseases.

Other cards nod to his hobbies. He has trained in jiu-jitsu and entered real tournaments, rides electric hydrofoil boards, raises cattle on a Hawaiian ranch and has said he wears the same gray shirt most days so he has one less choice to make. Prices for the estate and the shelter are rough guesses.
