## How To Play Draw a Map From Memory

Each round gives you 6 places to draw. You see a name, like Italy or Greenland, and a blank box with a dotted frame. Your job is to sketch the outline of that place without peeking at a map.

Draw with your mouse, trackpad or finger. You can use as many strokes as you like, so it is fine to lift your pen and go back for an island. Press **Undo** to remove your last stroke or **Clear** to start over. When you are happy, press **Done**.

Now the real shape slides over your drawing in green, and you get a score out of 100%. If you have no idea what a place looks like, press **Skip** to see the real outline. A skipped map scores 0, but at least you learn something for next time.

After 6 maps you see your average and a mini gallery of every drawing next to the real thing.

## What Your Score Means

Your score shows how close your outline is to the real one once both are lined up and scaled to the same size.

- **90% and up:** almost a perfect trace. You must stare at maps for fun.
- **70% to 89%:** clearly the right place, with a few coasts in odd spots.
- **45% to 69%:** the basic shape is there, but some parts are stretched or missing.
- **Under 45%:** more of a mystery island than a real country.

Long thin places like Chile reward getting the shape right more than the details. Places with lots of islands, like Japan, lose points if you forget a big island.

## Tips To Improve Your Map Drawing

- **Get the shape of the box right first.** Is the place tall and thin, wide and flat, or roughly round? Proportions matter more than tiny bays.
- **Start with a landmark.** Italy's boot, Florida's thumb on the United States, or the bump of West Africa give you a point to build the rest around.
- **Remember the islands.** Sicily, Tasmania and the two halves of New Zealand all count.
- **Draw big.** A larger sketch gives your hand more room for detail.
- **Use Skip to study.** Looking closely at a shape you don't know is the fastest way to learn it.

## Why Maps Are So Hard to Draw

Most of us see maps all the time, but we rarely look at them closely. Your brain keeps a rough "mental map" that is great for knowing that Italy is in Europe, but fuzzy on where its heel and toe really point.

Many world maps also stretch places near the poles. On those maps Greenland looks almost as big as Africa, when Africa is really about 14 times larger. That is why every place in this game is scaled to fill the box, so you are judged on shape, not size.

Chile is another surprise for many people. It is about 4,300 kilometers long but, on average, less than 200 kilometers wide, so most first tries come out far too chubby. When you want a break from coastlines, [connect the stars and draw a constellation](/draw-a-constellation/) instead.
